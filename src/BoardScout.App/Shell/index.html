What each screen does

      
    
        
  • Overview: the board as Linux reports it: CPU, memory slots, PCIe lanes and M.2, SATA ports, USB, and the chipset. Hover anything.
    • 
        
  • Overview: the board as Windows reports it: CPU, memory slots, graphics and M.2 lanes, SATA, USB, and the chipset. Hover anything.
    • 
        
  • Topology: what connects straight to the CPU and what shares the chipset's link.
    • 
        
  • Connections: every device and the link it negotiated, live traffic, Live drives, search (Ctrl+F or /), and the Optimization plan.
    • 
        
  • Drivers: the kernel driver behind each device, and firmware versions; Check firmware asks fwupd what's waiting.
    • 
        
  • Drivers: each component's driver; Check drivers looks for newer ones on vendor and OEM pages and links to them.
    • 
        
  • System: what your distribution says about you, its support dates, a security check, packages, history, and timers.
    • 
        
  • System: Windows' personality, support dates, upgrade readiness, updates, apps, and scheduled tasks.
    • 
        
  • Machines: other PCs and servers. Open one's dashboard, or check them all at once, over SSH with your own keys.
    • 
      

      
This dashboard is on another machine

      
    
        
  • A BoardScout on your computer started it over SSH. It serves only that machine itself, and you reach it through the SSH connection.
    • 
        
  • Disconnect (in Diagnostics) or closing the dashboard on your computer stops it. It also stops on its own after a few idle minutes.
    • 
        
  • Its copy and settings live in ~/.cache/boardscout%LOCALAPPDATA%\BoardScout\Remote on that machine.
    • 
      

      
Running BoardScout

      
    
        
  • It runs in your browser and serves only this computer by default. There's no tray icon: stop it here (Diagnostics → Stop BoardScout) or with Ctrl+C where it runs.
    • 
        
  • boardscout --install adds it to your app menu; that entry stops BoardScout when its last tab closes.
    • 
        
  • On a server: boardscout --listen 0.0.0.0, then open the printed link from another computer. Anyone with that link can see this machine's details.
    • 
        
  • This is BoardScout.exe --serve: the browser dashboard for this PC, without the app's window. Stop it here (Diagnostics → Stop BoardScout) or with Ctrl+C.
    • 
        
  • Other machines: the Machines tab, or --connect NAME and --check all from a terminal.
    • 
        
  • Nothing needs root or administrator rights. Running it again opens the dashboard that's already running.
    •
