What each screen does

      
    
        
  • Overview: the board as Linux reports it: CPU, memory slots, PCIe lanes and M.2, SATA ports, USB, and the chipset. Hover anything.
    • 
        
  • Topology: what connects straight to the CPU and what shares the chipset's link.
    • 
        
  • Connections: every device and the link it negotiated, live traffic, Live drives, search (Ctrl+F or /), and the Optimization plan.
    • 
        
  • Drivers: the kernel driver behind each device, and firmware versions; Check firmware asks fwupd what's waiting.
    • 
        
  • System: what your distribution says about you, its support dates, a security check, packages, history, and timers.
    • 
      

      
Running BoardScout

      
    
        
  • It runs in your browser and serves only this computer by default. There's no tray icon: stop it here (Diagnostics → Stop BoardScout) or with Ctrl+C where it runs.
    • 
        
  • boardscout --install adds it to your app menu; that entry stops BoardScout when its last tab closes.
    • 
        
  • On a server: boardscout --listen 0.0.0.0, then open the printed link from another computer. Anyone with that link can see this machine's details.
    • 
        
  • Nothing needs root. Running it again opens the dashboard that's already running.
    • 
      

      
What goes online

      
    
        
  • Only when you click: Look up my public IP (one request to Cloudflare's 1.1.1.1) and Test my Internet speed (speed.cloudflare.com). The Scan Log records both.
    • 
        
  • The Connections map talks to your local network only: your router, its web certificate, and devices' own names (multicast DNS, NetBIOS, UPnP).
